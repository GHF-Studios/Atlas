# Writing Guide

Write to answer a question, not to fill a template. Let the topic, evidence, disagreement, and uncertainty decide how much room it needs: one subject may take a few lines, another several pages. Consistency means being clear about what you know and why—not making every page the same length.

Use folder nesting for distinct topics. A folder's README should orient the reader to that topic; paragraphs can explain connected reasoning, bullets can hold separate points, and links can lead to related subjects. Do not repeat whole accounts or add sections just to make a page look complete.

For important claims, make clear whether they are observed, intended, inferred, remembered, or unresolved. Link to primary sources and include dates where they matter. The [Sources and Evidence](../03-sources-and-evidence/README.md) guide discusses what different sources can establish.

Atlas is intended to be human-authored or substantially human-reviewed. This guide is still a proposal: there is no required template, minimum length, or fixed ratio of prose to bullets.
