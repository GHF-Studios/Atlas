# Document Statuses

A document status should tell readers where a page is in its writing and review—not whether every claim in it is true, whether a proposal was adopted, or whether software works. Those questions need evidence and the right source; see [Sources and Evidence](../03-sources-and-evidence/README.md).

If Atlas adopts a small set of labels, this could be a starting point:

- **Placeholder:** a structural home with little or no substantive content.
- **Draft:** content exists, but research or writing is incomplete, or review is still pending.
- **Reviewed:** a person has checked the page as current at a particular point; it remains revisable.
- **Superseded:** a newer page or decision replaces it; link to the replacement where possible.

I suggest **Reviewed** instead of the earlier candidate **Established**: it says what happened to the page without implying that its claims are settled facts. This vocabulary and any metadata format remain unadopted. Do not confuse page status with product maturity or deployment status; see the separate [Status Labels](../../08-reference/documentation/status-labels/README.md) note.
